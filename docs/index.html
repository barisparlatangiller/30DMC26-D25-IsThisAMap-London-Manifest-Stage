Is a stage a map?

Manifest · Intro & Hileli · live at OVO Arena Wembley, London

Manifest is a six-member Turkish girl group. I followed all six through one live song, every 2 seconds, and put them on a stage plan in metres. So the song draws its own map, like the heat maps of players in a football match.

Method & facts

How the song became a map

Every 2 seconds, the position of each performer was read from the concert video ( direct observations, the rest interpolated) and placed on an approximate 24 × 24 m stage plan: a raised platform at +3.3 m with stairs, the main stage, a 7.5 m runway and an 18 m front block. Between the 2 s samples the performers glide on a smooth curve. Who stands in front of whom was read again from the wide shots every 2.5 s; the backing dancers seen in those shots are drawn as grey figures, not mapped one by one.

Colour: every sample counts as 2 seconds of presence, strongest where she stands and gone 3.2 m away. Fresh footprints glow, then settle to a lasting trace. Where several performers share the floor, the spot takes the colour of whoever stood there longest; it gets brighter the more time was spent there in total. On top, every stop leaves a footprint: a circle in her colour wherever she stayed within 0.9 m, its area growing with the time she stood there (2 s small, 30 s about 2 m across). Stops of 20 s or more carry their duration. From 0:45, when the six can be told apart, every position counts, also while the camera looks elsewhere.

Sync with the video

The positions were noted on a clock that drifts against the published video, so the map follows the video through ten anchor moments checked by eye (platform, stairs, main stage, the solo, the regroup, the run, the front block, the final pose). On 24 Nov every passage from 0:45 to the final pose was checked again against the video at ¼ speed, formation by formation. If the map runs ahead or behind on your screen, nudge it:

The stage around the map is drawn after the video: the LED wall, the truss and moving lights, the edge light strips, the CO₂ jets at the moments they fire, the screens beside the stage (a live close-up camera of this 3D stage) and the crowd. The six are stylised figures in their own hair and costume colours. None of this is data; the colours on the floor and the positions are.
