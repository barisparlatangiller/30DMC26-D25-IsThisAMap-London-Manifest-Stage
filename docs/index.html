Method & facts

How the song became a map

Every 2 seconds, the position of each performer was read from the concert video ( direct observations, the rest interpolated) and placed on an approximate 24 × 24 m stage plan: a raised platform at +3.3 m with stairs, the main stage, a 7.5 m runway and an 18 m front block. Between the 2 s samples the performers glide on a smooth curve. Who stands in front of whom was read again from the wide shots every 2.5 s; the backing dancers seen in those shots are drawn as grey figures, not mapped one by one.

Colour: every sample counts as 2 seconds of presence, strongest where she stands and gone 3.2 m away. Fresh footprints glow, then settle to a lasting trace. Where several performers share the floor, their colours blend into each other, weighted by how long each one stood there. Clear sightings count fully, approximate ones 0.6, silhouettes 0.4; moments when she is out of frame are left out and shown as a dashed ring.

Sync with the video

The positions were noted on a clock that drifts against the published video (about 3 s at the start, 40 s near the end), so the map follows the video through six anchor moments checked by eye. If the map runs ahead or behind on your screen, nudge it:
